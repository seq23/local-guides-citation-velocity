Direct answers

No-screening or low-screening intake is a major problem

A clinic should be able to explain who is and is not a good candidate before treatment starts. Minimal screening is not a convenience feature.

Monitoring and side-effect planning should be concrete

Safety depends on how the clinic tracks side effects, symptoms, and lab changes over time. This should sound like a protocol, not a promise to “check in later.”

Fertility and long-term tradeoffs should not be brushed aside

If fertility or future family planning matters, that should be part of the initial decision conversation. A clinic that ignores it is telling you how it thinks about risk.

Quick answers

A clinic should be able to explain who is and is not a good candidate before treatment starts. Minimal screening is not a convenience feature.

Quick checklist

  • Ask what disqualifies someone or triggers caution
  • Ask what baseline evaluation is required
  • Ask whether symptoms alone are enough to start
  • Low-risk profile: stable baseline labs, good follow-up, and no obvious pressure points ignored
  • Medium-risk profile: symptoms or history that need tighter monitoring and clearer dose-change rules
  • Higher-risk profile: blood pressure, sleep, fertility, hematocrit, or other history that changes the plan
  • Monitoring rule: know what will be checked, when, and what triggers action

Red flags

  • They are willing to prescribe before meaningful review
  • Treats every patient profile the same
  • No monitoring schedule
  • Minimizes blood pressure, sleep, or fertility issues
  • No action thresholds for abnormal labs
  • Safety talk only appears after payment
