SSDI evaluation vs school IEP evaluation vs forensic evaluation

An SSDI evaluation, a school IEP evaluation and a forensic evaluation are assessments that answer different decision-makers' questions: disability eligibility, educational services or a legal issue. The referral question determines the evidence and the evaluator needed, so name the decision-maker and the question before you book.

Quick answer

The referral question determines the evidence and evaluator needed. On decision-maker, SSDI evaluation: Social Security Administration; School IEP or forensic evaluation: school IEP team, or a court or attorney for forensic work. On question answered, SSDI evaluation: whether impairments prevent substantial work; School IEP or forensic evaluation: school: eligibility for special education; forensic: a legal question.

Direct answers

Which decision does each of these three evaluations serve?

These evaluations answer different decision-maker questions: disability eligibility, educational services, or a legal/forensic issue. The referral question determines the evidence and evaluator needed.

When is an SSDI evaluation needed?

An SSDI evaluation is needed when Social Security needs more medical evidence to decide a disability claim. SSA may schedule a consultative exam, and treating providers' records also carry weight.

When are school or forensic evaluations needed?

A school IEP evaluation is needed to decide special education eligibility for a student. A forensic evaluation is needed when a court or attorney asks a legal question, such as competency, custody or injury in a lawsuit.
