Direct answers

Which citizenship test will I take in 2026?

Your Form N-400 filing date decides it, not the calendar year of your interview. USCIS states that aliens who file Form N-400, Application for Naturalization, before Oct. 20, 2025 will take the 2008 naturalization civics test, and that aliens who file on or after Oct. 20, 2025 will take the 2025 naturalization civics test. USCIS says it is implementing the 2025 test to align with Executive Order 14161, and that the 2025 test is based on the 2020 test with some modifications to the administration of the test. So there is no single "citizenship test 2026". If you file your N-400 during 2026, you take the 2025 test. If you filed before Oct. 20, 2025 and your interview lands in 2026, you take the 2008 test. Read the filing date off your receipt notice before you buy or download anything.

What is on the 2025 naturalization civics test?

USCIS describes the 2025 civics test as an oral test consisting of 20 questions from the list of 128 civics test questions. You must answer 12 questions correctly to pass. You fail if you answer 9 of the 20 questions incorrectly. Officers stop asking questions once an applicant has answered 12 correctly or 9 incorrectly, so the test can end before all 20 questions are asked. USCIS publishes the official list as "128 Civics Questions and Answers" and a companion study guide titled "One Nation, One People: The USCIS 2025 Civics Test Study Guide". USCIS also notes that some answers change because of elections or appointments, and that you must answer with the name of the official serving at the time of your naturalization interview.

What is on the 2008 civics test, and who still takes it in 2026?

The 2008 test still governs anyone who filed Form N-400 before Oct. 20, 2025, which includes people whose interviews fall during 2026. USCIS describes it as an oral test in which the officer asks up to 10 questions from the list of 100 civics test questions. You must answer 6 questions correctly to pass, and you fail the test if you answer 5 of the 10 questions incorrectly. As with the 2025 test, officers stop asking once an applicant answers 6 correctly or 5 incorrectly. The official list is published by USCIS as "100 Civics Questions and Answers", with a large-print edition and a text-only edition that USCIS says can be used with screen enlargers, screen readers, refreshable Braille displays, and most other accessibility products.

USCIS describes the same English requirement alongside both civics versions: you must demonstrate an understanding of the English language, including the ability to read, write, and speak basic English. For the reading test you read one of three sentences out loud correctly. For the writing test you write one of three sentences correctly. Your ability to speak and understand English is determined by a USCIS officer during the eligibility interview on Form N-400, not as a separate sitting. The USCIS Policy Manual adds the detail that changes how people should prepare: the officer stops the reading test once you read one of the three sentences correctly, and stops the writing test once you write one of the three sentences in a manner the officer understands. The Policy Manual also states that an applicant does not fail the writing test because of spelling, capitalization, or punctuation errors, unless the errors interfere with the meaning of the sentence and the officer is unable to understand it.

Quick checklist

  • Practise reading aloud, because the reading test is scored on whether the officer can understand the meaning.
  • Use the official USCIS writing vocabulary list rather than a general English word list.
  • Remember the speaking test happens throughout the N-400 interview, not at a separate moment.
  • Ask the officer to repeat or rephrase a question; the Policy Manual says officers should repeat and rephrase.

Red flags

  • A tutor tells you a spelling mistake automatically fails the writing test.
  • A preparer treats the speaking test as a separate scheduled exam.
  • A practice program never has you read a sentence out loud.

Two. The USCIS Policy Manual states that an alien has two opportunities to pass the English and civics tests: the initial examination and the re-examination interview. USCIS denies the naturalization application if the applicant fails to pass any portion of the tests after two attempts. Where an applicant requests a USCIS hearing on the denial, officers must administer any failed portion of the tests. One rule catches people out and is worth reading twice: unless USCIS excuses it, failing to appear at the re-examination for testing, or failing to take the tests at an examination or hearing, counts as a failed attempt to pass the test. A missed appointment is not a neutral event.

Quick checklist

  • Treat the re-examination interview as your second and final attempt.
  • If you cannot attend a re-examination, contact USCIS before the date rather than after.
  • Keep every appointment notice, because non-appearance counts as a failed attempt unless USCIS excuses it.
  • If your application is denied, ask what a USCIS hearing would involve, since officers must administer any failed portion at that hearing.

Red flags

  • You are told there is no limit on retakes.
  • A missed re-examination is described as harmless.
  • Nobody explains that failing any portion after two attempts leads to denial.

The USCIS Policy Manual sets out the exceptions under INA 312(b). If you are age 50 or older at the time of filing and have resided in the United States as a lawful permanent resident for at least 20 years, you are exempt from the English requirement but still required to meet the civics requirement, and you may take the civics test in the language of your choice using an interpreter. The same English exemption applies if you are age 55 or older with at least 15 years as an LPR. If you are age 65 or older with at least 20 years as an LPR, you receive special consideration on the civics test: USCIS says it administers a test of 10 questions from a specially selected bank of 20 test questions drawn from either the 2008 or the 2025 civics test, marked with an asterisk, and that you may take the naturalization test in the language of your choice. Separately, an applicant who cannot meet the English or civics requirements because of a medical disability may be exempt from the English requirement, the civics requirement, or both, using Form N-648.

Quick checklist

  • Count your age and your years as an LPR at the time of filing, which is the moment the Policy Manual measures.
  • If you qualify for an English exemption, arrange an interpreter for the civics test.
  • If you qualify under 65/20, study only the 20 questions marked with an asterisk on your version of the list.
  • Discuss Form N-648 with a licensed medical professional if a disability prevents you meeting either requirement.

Red flags

  • Someone measures your age or LPR years from your interview date rather than your filing date.
  • A preparer offers a medical disability exception without a qualifying medical professional completing Form N-648.
  • You are told the 65/20 special consideration removes the civics requirement entirely; USCIS says the civics requirement is still required with specially designated test forms.

No, and confusing the two costs people time. The English and civics tests belong to Form N-400, Application for Naturalization, and are administered by a USCIS officer at the naturalization interview. The immigration medical examination is a separate step recorded on Form I-693, Report of Immigration Medical Examination and Vaccination Record, and it is performed by a physician USCIS has designated as a civil surgeon. USCIS policy guidance treats the medical examination as a question of admissibility, and applicants most often meet it alongside Form I-485, Application to Register Permanent Residence or Adjust Status, the green-card step that usually falls years before naturalization. Many people go through both, but they are different forms, different examiners, and different timelines. Nothing on the civics test is medical, and no civil surgeon administers it.

Quick checklist

  • Keep the N-400 naturalization track and the I-693 medical track as separate checklists.
  • Use the USCIS civil surgeon locator only for the Form I-693 medical examination.
  • Do not expect a civil surgeon appointment to satisfy anything on the naturalization test.
  • Check which form each deadline belongs to before you act on it.

Red flags

  • A preparer bundles the naturalization test and the immigration medical examination into a single appointment.
  • You are told a civil surgeon can sign off on English or civics.
  • A timeline mixes I-485 medical deadlines with N-400 interview scheduling.
