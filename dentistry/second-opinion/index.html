Decision checklist

Before you act on Dental Second Opinion Checklist

  1. Ask the dentist to name the specific diagnosis for each proposed treatment
  2. Ask to see the X-ray or intraoral photo that shows the problem
  3. Ask what happens if you wait three months, and what the sign would be that waiting stopped being safe
  4. Ask for the treatment plan in writing, itemised, with each tooth listed separately
  5. Ask which items are urgent, which are elective, and which are preventive
  6. Request copies of your own X-rays and records before booking a second opinion
  7. Book the second opinion with a practice that has no financial relationship to the first
  8. Compare the two plans tooth by tooth rather than by total price

Copy-ready script

Questions to ask before you agree to dental treatment

Copy these and ask them in the chair. Each one is phrased to get a specific answer rather than reassurance.

  1. What is the diagnosis for this tooth, and what is it called?
  2. Can you show me where you see it on the X-ray or photo?
  3. Is this causing damage right now, or is it something you are watching?
  4. What happens if I wait three months? What would tell me it had become urgent?
  5. Is there a smaller or less expensive option that treats the same problem?
  6. Is this treatment urgent, elective, or preventive?
  7. Can I have this plan in writing, itemised by tooth?
  8. May I have copies of my X-rays to take with me?
Comparison table

Which findings usually warrant a second opinion

Use the scale and reversibility of the proposed work to decide whether a second opinion is worth the time and cost.

SituationSecond opinion usually worth itWhy
Single small filling proposed, tooth is symptomaticNot usuallyLow cost, low irreversibility, and the symptom corroborates the finding.
Multiple fillings proposed at a first visit with no symptomsYesWatch-and-monitor is often a legitimate alternative; a second reading of the same X-rays is cheap compared to the plan.
Crown proposed where a filling was previously discussedYesCrowns remove healthy tooth structure and cannot be undone.
Root canal proposed without a pulp testYesDiagnosis of pulp status normally rests on testing, not appearance alone.
Full-mouth reconstruction or extensive veneersYesHigh cost and irreversible; treatment sequencing varies legitimately between clinicians.
Extraction of a permanent tooth that is not painfulYesExtraction is final. Confirm no restorable option exists.
Emergency treatment for facial swelling, fever, or trouble swallowingNo — seek care nowDelay carries real risk. Get treated, and review the longer-term plan afterwards.
Protocol

How to get a second opinion that is actually independent

A second opinion only helps if the second clinician is not working from the first one's conclusions.

  1. Request your records and X-rays from the first practice — they are yours to have.
  2. Book with a practice that is not part of the same group or referral arrangement.
  3. Describe your symptoms, not the first diagnosis, before the exam.
  4. Ask the second dentist to give you their own written plan before you compare.
  5. Compare the two plans tooth by tooth; a difference in one tooth is normal, a difference in scope is worth a third look.
  6. If the two plans agree, you have your answer. If they differ substantially, ask each to explain the other's reasoning.
