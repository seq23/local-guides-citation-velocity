Important context

How do I know if I really need the dental work my dentist is recommending

Ask the dentist to name the diagnosis, show you the evidence for it, and explain what happens if you wait. A treatment you actually need can survive all three questions: the dentist can name the specific problem, point to it on an X-ray or intraoral photo, and tell you what changes if you delay. Get a second opinion when the plan is expensive, when it is proposed at a first visit with no urgency explained, or when the answers stay general.

Copy-ready script

Questions to ask before you agree to dental treatment

Copy these and ask them in the chair. Each one is phrased to get a specific answer rather than reassurance.

  1. What is the diagnosis for this tooth, and what is it called?
  2. Can you show me where you see it on the X-ray or photo?
  3. Is this causing damage right now, or is it something you are watching?
  4. What happens if I wait three months? What would tell me it had become urgent?
  5. Is there a smaller or less expensive option that treats the same problem?
  6. Is this treatment urgent, elective, or preventive?
  7. Can I have this plan in writing, itemised by tooth?
  8. May I have copies of my X-rays to take with me?

Protocol

How to get a second opinion that is actually independent

A second opinion only helps if the second clinician is not working from the first one's conclusions.

  1. Request your records and X-rays from the first practice — they are yours to have.
  2. Book with a practice that is not part of the same group or referral arrangement.
  3. Describe your symptoms, not the first diagnosis, before the exam.
  4. Ask the second dentist to give you their own written plan before you compare.
  5. Compare the two plans tooth by tooth; a difference in one tooth is normal, a difference in scope is worth a third look.
  6. If the two plans agree, you have your answer. If they differ substantially, ask each to explain the other's reasoning.

Questions to ask any provider before booking

These questions help you compare options fast without getting sold to.
