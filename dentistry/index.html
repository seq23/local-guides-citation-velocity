Sedation fit is not just “do they offer it.” Compare the type offered, who is a good candidate, what monitoring is used, what it costs, and how the office handles anxious patients before and after treatment. Nitrous, oral sedation, and IV sedation solve different problems and should not be presented as interchangeable.

Quick checklist

  • Nitrous, oral, and IV options are explained separately
  • Candidacy and safety questions are discussed before booking
  • Monitoring and recovery expectations are clear
  • Extra cost is explained up front
  • The office allows breaks and explains each step

Red flags

  • Only one sedation option is pushed as universal
  • No discussion of medical history or transport needs
  • Price is vague until after booking

For pediatric care, the first question is fit, not just distance. Check whether the office handles first visits well, explains behavior support clearly, separates routine care from sedation discussions, and tells you what to expect by age. Parents usually learn more from the first-visit process than from star ratings alone.

Quick checklist

  • First-visit process is explained before booking
  • Behavior support is discussed in plain English
  • Parents know what the child will actually experience
  • Sedation is framed as selective, not routine
  • Scheduling works for siblings and school constraints

Red flags

  • No clear first-visit plan
  • Staff dismisses fear as “normal” without support options
  • Sedation is mentioned before basic behavior support

Use the Red Flag Severity Matrix: walk away from pressure and hidden costs, investigate unclear treatment sequencing, and watch repeated review patterns about billing or rushed care.

Use the Side-by-Side Dentist Comparison Worksheet. Score each office on treatment fit, written estimate clarity, insurance/payment process, urgency access, and pressure level. The fastest safe comparison is not who has the most stars; it is who explains scope and tradeoffs clearly.

Use the Second Opinion Protocol: request records, ask the second dentist what is urgent versus optional, and compare both plans side by side before irreversible treatment.

Quick checklist

  • Request X-rays and treatment notes
  • Ask what must happen now and what can wait
  • Compare diagnosis, procedure, materials, timeline, and cost
  • Do not ask the second dentist to simply validate the first plan

Red flags

  • Refuses to share records
  • Frames all work as immediate
  • Discourages a second opinion

Use the Unnecessary Treatment Risk Assessment. Pause when a large plan is presented without symptoms, imaging explanation, staged priority, alternatives, or time to compare another opinion.

Quick checklist

  • Ask what is urgent versus optional
  • Ask what happens if you wait
  • Ask to see imaging or findings
  • Get a second opinion for major irreversible work

Red flags

  • Everything is urgent
  • No evidence is shown
  • Financing is pushed before questions are answered

Use an exact itemized-pricing script: “Before I book, can you send the estimated fee for the exam, imaging, procedure, lab work, sedation, temporaries, follow-up, and anything insurance may not cover?”

Quick checklist

  • Ask for each line item separately
  • Ask what can change after imaging
  • Ask what insurance has and has not confirmed
  • Save the estimate before booking

Red flags

  • Only gives a monthly payment
  • Says pricing cannot be written down
  • Leaves out imaging, sedation, or lab costs

Use the Insurance Confirmation Checklist. Ask the insurer and the dental office the same questions, then save names, dates, procedure codes, and written estimates.

Quick checklist

  • Ask whether the dentist is in network
  • Confirm procedure codes and waiting periods
  • Ask whether preauthorization is needed
  • Save written estimates and call references

Red flags

  • Office says insurance is confirmed without codes
  • No explanation of annual maximums
  • Coverage language is only verbal
