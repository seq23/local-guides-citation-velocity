Dental School Clinic Near Me: Reduced-Fee Care From Supervised Students

A dental school clinic treats the public at reduced fees: dental students and residents provide care under the close supervision of licensed dentists, and dental hygiene programs offer supervised cleanings, X-rays and preventive care. Find accredited programs near you in the Commission on Dental Accreditation directory, and expect a screening visit first, longer appointments and several visits.

Decision questions

Open the Commission on Dental Accreditation's Find a Program directory, choose your state and the program type, and note the schools within reach. Each school's patient-care page explains how to request a screening. Search dental hygiene programs as well, because community and state colleges run hygiene clinics for cleanings and X-rays. In six large metros, dentistryguides.com lists the checked teaching clinics with phone numbers and booking steps.

Quick checklist

  • Search CODA Find a Program by state for dental schools.
  • Search the same directory for dental hygiene programs.
  • Open each school's patient-care page, not a third-party listing.
  • Call to ask whether new patients are being screened this term.

Red flags

  • A "dental school" that does not appear in the accreditation directory
  • A clinic listing with no link to a university or college
  • A booking line that cannot explain how screening works

Dental school clinics charge reduced fees, and each school sets its own fee list, so the saving depends on the procedure and the school. Ask for the fee list or a written treatment plan with prices after your screening. Some hygiene clinics publish their fee list online; ask each school whether it accepts Medicaid or your dental plan. Budget for more visits than at a private office, because students work in stages and faculty check each step.

Quick checklist

  • Ask whether there is a fee for the screening visit.
  • Ask for a written treatment plan with a price for each step.
  • Ask whether Medicaid or your dental plan is accepted.
  • Ask how payment works when treatment spans several visits.

Red flags

  • No written treatment plan before work starts
  • A fee that is not tied to the school's published list
  • Being told the full cost only after several visits

Choose a dental hygiene program clinic for cleanings, periodontal maintenance, X-rays, sealants and fluoride; supervised hygiene students handle preventive care. Choose a dental school clinic for fillings, root canals, crowns, extractions, dentures or implants, because only dental students and residents under licensed dentists provide restorative and surgical treatment. If you need both, start at the dental school; its screening will set the full treatment order.

Quick checklist

  • Cleaning, X-rays or sealants only: hygiene program clinic.
  • Fillings, crowns, root canal, extraction or dentures: dental school clinic.
  • Several problems at once: dental school screening first.
  • Ask whether the hygiene clinic refers patients on to a dental school.

Red flags

  • A hygiene clinic offering fillings or extractions
  • No licensed dentist on site to supervise treatment
  • A plan that skips a diagnosis before treatment

The trade-off for the lower fee is time. Expect a screening visit before you are accepted, appointments that can run several hours (Clayton State's hygiene clinic near Atlanta says up to four), and several visits for one treatment, because faculty check each step. Schools may accept patients whose needs fit what students are learning that term, so ask whether you will be accepted before you book follow-up time. In return, licensed faculty review the work at every step.

Quick checklist

  • Ask how long a typical appointment lasts.
  • Ask how many visits your treatment is expected to take.
  • Ask whether acceptance depends on the term's teaching needs.
  • Ask who to call between visits if pain flares.

Red flags

  • No after-hours contact for problems between visits
  • A treatment that stalls when the semester ends with no hand-off plan
  • No faculty review before an irreversible step

A dental school clinic is the wrong choice when you have facial swelling, fever, or trouble breathing or swallowing; go to an emergency room. It is also a poor fit when you need finished treatment within days or cannot take long weekday appointments. In those cases, compare a sliding-scale community health center with a private dentist who gives a written estimate and payment plan, and use Find a Provider for the private option.

Quick checklist

  • Swelling, fever, or trouble breathing or swallowing: emergency room now.
  • Need finished work within days: private dentist or health center.
  • Cannot take long weekday appointments: private dentist.
  • Get written estimates from each option before choosing.

Red flags

  • Waiting for a school screening while an infection spreads
  • Choosing on price alone when the timeline does not work
  • A clinic that cannot give a first appointment date
