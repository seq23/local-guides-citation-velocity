Free Dental Clinics Near Me: How to Find Free and Low-Cost Dental Care

A free or low-cost dental clinic near you is one of three kinds: a federally funded community health center that charges on a sliding scale by income, a public health department dental clinic, or a volunteer charity clinic. Find community health centers with HRSA's Find a Health Center search, then call to confirm eligibility, documents and cost before you go.

Decision questions

Search HRSA's Find a Health Center by address; federally funded health centers are in most cities and many rural areas and charge on a sliding scale by income. Then check your county health department for a public dental clinic and look for a volunteer charity clinic. In Atlanta, Chicago, Dallas, Houston, Los Angeles and Miami, dentistryguides.com lists the checked options with address, phone and cost.

Quick checklist

  • Search Find a Health Center with your home address, not just your city.
  • Filter the results for centers that list dental services.
  • Check the county health department for a public dental clinic.
  • Open the dentistryguides.com city list if you live in one of the six covered metros.

Red flags

  • A "free clinic" listing with no address, phone or clinic website
  • A directory that charges you to see clinic contact details
  • A clinic that will not say who is eligible before you travel

Charity clinics usually require low household income, no dental insurance and residence in the area they serve. Atlanta's Ben Massell Dental Clinic, for example, sees uninsured adults in 13 metro counties with household income at or below 125% of the Federal Poverty Level. Community health centers see everyone and set the fee by income, so they are the right door when you earn too much for a charity clinic.

Quick checklist

  • Write down household size and monthly income before you call.
  • Ask whether the clinic serves your county or ZIP code.
  • Ask whether Medicaid or Medicare members are seen or sent to their plan.
  • Ask for the income limit as a percentage of the Federal Poverty Level.

Red flags

  • An income limit that is not stated anywhere
  • Being told you qualify without any eligibility check
  • A waiting list with no estimated date

A federally funded community health center sets your fee from your household income and size, so two patients can pay different amounts for the same filling. Bring proof of income and ask for the fee tier you fall in before treatment starts. Ask for a written estimate for anything beyond an exam, cleaning and X-rays, because crowns, root canals and dentures can carry lab fees on top of the visit fee.

Quick checklist

  • Ask which sliding-fee tier your income places you in.
  • Ask what the exam, X-rays and cleaning cost at that tier.
  • Get a written estimate before any crown, root canal, extraction or denture.
  • Ask whether lab fees are billed separately.

Red flags

  • A quote that changes after treatment has started
  • Lab or material fees that were not mentioned up front
  • Pressure to sign a financing agreement at the chair

Bring a photo ID, proof of address dated within the last 30 days, proof of income for every adult in the household and any insurance or Medicaid card. Many charity clinics will not schedule a first visit until those documents arrive; Ben Massell in Atlanta asks for them by email before booking. Add a list of your medicines and any recent dental X-rays so the first visit is not spent repeating them.

Quick checklist

  • Photo ID for the patient.
  • Proof of address from the last 30 days, such as official mail.
  • Proof of income for every adult in the household.
  • Medicine list, insurance or Medicaid card, and recent X-rays.

Red flags

  • Being asked to pay a cash deposit to join a charity clinic waiting list
  • A request for your Social Security number with no explanation
  • Being asked to send documents to a personal email address

A free clinic is the wrong first stop for facial swelling, fever, or trouble breathing or swallowing; go to an emergency room. It is also slow when you need finished work soon, because charity clinics often run waiting lists. If you have Medicaid dental coverage, book through your plan's dentist network. If you can pay a modest amount and need treatment this month, compare a sliding-scale health center with a private dentist offering a written payment plan.

Quick checklist

  • Swelling, fever, or trouble breathing or swallowing: emergency room now.
  • Medicaid dental coverage: book inside your plan network.
  • Need treatment within weeks: compare a health center and a private dentist side by side.
  • Ask each option for a written estimate and timeline.

Red flags

  • Waiting weeks for a charity slot while swelling spreads
  • A clinic that cannot say when treatment would finish
  • Advice to skip an emergency room for a spreading infection
