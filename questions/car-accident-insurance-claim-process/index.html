How does the car accident insurance claim process work?

A car accident insurance claim process is the formal request you submit to an insurance company to pay for vehicle repairs and medical costs after a crash. You share details of the event, and the company reviews the facts to decide how much money they will provide.

Published 2 November 2026 · The Industry Guides

Published by The Industry Guides Editorial Team.

What is the first step after a crash?

The process starts right after the collision happens. You gather details at the scene. You take photos of the vehicles and the road. You get the other driver's name, phone number, and policy details. You write down the names of any witnesses. If police come to the scene, you ask for a copy of the report.

Next, you contact your own insurance company to report the crash. You share the basic facts of what happened. You tell them where and when the crash took place. You do not need to guess about who was at fault during this first call. You just report the facts as you know them.

Many drivers also look into medical care right away. If you feel pain, you should see a doctor. Insurance companies look at your medical records to understand your injuries. For more details on this step, you can read how long do i have to see a doctor after a car accident for a claim on this site.

After you report the crash, an insurance adjuster is assigned to your case. This person investigates the event. They review the police report and look at your photos. They may call you to ask questions about how the crash happened. You should answer honestly and stick to the plain facts.

How do adjusters investigate the claim?

The insurance adjuster looks at all the evidence. They talk to both drivers to hear different sides of the story. They look at the damage to the vehicles. Sometimes they visit the scene of the crash. They want to figure out who caused the collision. This helps them decide which insurance policy pays for the costs.

If your car needs repairs, an adjuster or an inspector will look at it. They write an estimate of the repair costs. You can usually take your car to a shop of your choice. The shop and the insurance company will talk about the cost of the parts and labor needed to fix the damage.

Medical bills are also part of the review. The adjuster asks for your medical bills and records from your doctors. They check these records to see which treatments connect directly to the crash. You must keep copies of all bills and receipts you receive.

It helps to stay organized during this time. Keep a folder for all letters, emails, and notes from phone calls. Write down the date and time of every chat you have with an insurance agent. This helps you track the progress of your claim without confusion.

What happens during the review and offer stage?

Once the investigation is done, the insurance company decides on a payout. They review the cost of fixing your car and your medical bills. They send you a letter with their decision. If they accept the claim, they offer a specific amount of money to cover your losses.

You do not have to accept the first offer. You can review the numbers to see if they cover all your actual costs. If the offer is too low, you can talk to the adjuster. You share more records or receipts to show why you need a higher amount to cover your repair or medical bills.

Sometimes, an insurance company decides to deny a claim. They might say the policy does not cover the specific type of crash, or they might dispute who caused it. If this happens, you can read Insurance denied claim — what now? on this site to learn about the next steps you can take.

If you want a deeper look at the whole timeline, you can read How to file a car accident claim on this site. It walks you through each stage from the moment you leave the scene until the final paperwork is signed and closed.

How does the claim process end?

The process ends when you and the insurance company agree on a final amount. You sign a release form. This form means you accept the money and agree not to ask for more cash for this specific crash in the future. Read the form carefully before you sign your name.

After you sign the release, the insurance company sends the payment. For car repairs, they might send a check directly to the repair shop or to you. For medical bills, the money goes to you or helps pay off the medical providers you used.

Keep in mind that this process takes time. Insurance companies have to review many documents. Delays can happen if records are missing or if the other driver does not return calls from the adjuster. Patience helps while you wait for the review to finish.

Remember that you are in control of your documents. You can ask questions if you do not understand a letter or a form. Take notes on every step so you always know where your claim stands.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to call my insurance company if the crash was small?

Yes, most insurance policies require you to report any crash promptly. If you wait, the company might deny coverage later. Reporting the crash helps protect you if the other driver changes their story or reports injuries days after the event.

What if the other driver does not have insurance?

Your own policy might include uninsured motorist coverage. This part of your policy helps pay for your car repairs and medical bills if the at-fault driver has no insurance. Check your policy documents to see if you have this specific coverage.

How long does the entire claim process take?

The time varies widely. Simple claims with minor car damage can finish in a few weeks. Complex claims involving serious injuries or disputes over fault can take many months. The speed depends on how fast documents and medical records are gathered.

Can I choose my own repair shop?

In most places, you have the right to pick the repair shop you want. The insurance company might suggest a preferred shop, but you can usually take your vehicle to a shop of your own choice for the repair estimate and work.
