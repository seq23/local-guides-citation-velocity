Direct answers

What is the core difference between dental insurance and medical insurance?

Dental and medical plans use different benefit designs, networks, exclusions, and claim rules. A service involving the mouth is not automatically covered by dental insurance, and medical coverage is not automatic either.

When does dental insurance usually pay?

Dental insurance usually pays for preventive care, fillings, crowns, root canals and similar treatment, often with waiting periods, frequency limits and a yearly maximum. Cosmetic work is usually excluded.

When might medical insurance pay for mouth care?

Medical insurance may pay when a mouth problem is caused by an accident, needs hospital care, or is tied to a medical condition, such as some jaw surgery or tooth removal before cancer treatment. Coverage depends on the plan's rules.
