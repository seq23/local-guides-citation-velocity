Civil surgeon vs panel physician

A civil surgeon is a doctor designated by USCIS to perform the immigration medical exam for adjustment of status inside the United States; a panel physician is a doctor appointed by the Department of State to perform it abroad. Which one you need depends on where you apply, so verify the current source before booking.

Quick answer

Use a civil surgeon for USCIS adjustment-of-status medical exams in the United States; panel physicians handle immigration medical exams abroad under Department of State processes. On where the exam happens, Civil surgeon: inside the United States; Panel physician: outside the United States, before the visa interview.

Direct answers

What is the core difference between a civil surgeon and a panel physician?

Use a civil surgeon for USCIS adjustment-of-status medical exams in the United States; panel physicians handle immigration medical exams abroad under Department of State processes.

When do I need a civil surgeon?

You need a civil surgeon when you are applying to adjust status to permanent resident while living in the United States. Only a doctor USCIS has designated as a civil surgeon can complete and sign Form I-693.

When do I need a panel physician?

You need a panel physician when your immigrant visa interview will take place at a U.S. embassy or consulate abroad. The Department of State lists the panel physicians each post accepts, and a doctor outside that list cannot do the exam.
