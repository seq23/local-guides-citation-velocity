What Is the USCIS Civil Surgeon Exam?

The civil surgeon examination is a medical evaluation required by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services (USCIS) for certain immigration benefits. Only physicians designated by USCIS as civil surgeons are authorized to conduct this exam and complete Form I-693. The sealed packet produced after the exam must be submitted to USCIS as part of your application â it cannot be opened by the applicant.

The exam typically covers a review of your medical history, a physical examination, required vaccinations, and tuberculosis screening, among other components. The civil surgeon then compiles the results, completes the official form, and provides a sealed envelope that you submit directly to USCIS or bring to your interview.

Why Costs Vary So Much

Unlike many government-set fees, civil surgeons set their own prices. This means the cost of the medical exam can differ significantly from one clinic to another â even within the same city. There is no federally mandated flat rate.

Another important distinction: some clinics quote an all-inclusive fee that covers the exam visit, required paperwork, and laboratory work. Others quote only the exam itself, with labs and administrative fees billed separately. This difference can make price comparisons misleading if you do not ask the right questions upfront.

Additional costs can arise if you are missing vaccinations, require follow-up tests, or need extra documentation. These are not always predictable at the time of booking.

Questions to Ask Before You Book

Before scheduling your civil surgeon appointment, it is strongly recommended that you get clear answers â in writing, when possible â to the following questions:

  • Does the quoted fee include paperwork and lab work, or only the exam visit?
  • What additional costs might I face, and under what circumstances?
  • How long after the exam will the sealed packet be ready?
  • What documents do I need to bring to the appointment?

Asking these questions in advance helps you compare clinics accurately and avoid unexpected expenses or timeline surprises.

What to Bring and Why Documentation Matters

Most civil surgeon offices will ask you to bring government-issued photo identification, your vaccination records, and any relevant USCIS paperwork related to your case. Vaccination history is particularly important â if records are incomplete, you may need to receive vaccines on-site or return for a follow-up appointment, both of which can add cost and delay.

Missing records are one of the most common reasons the process takes longer than expected, even when the appointment itself proceeds without issues. Gathering your documents thoroughly before your visit is one of the most effective ways to keep the process on track.

Understanding the Timeline

The general process follows this sequence: scheduling your appointment, attending the exam and submitting documents, completing any follow-up requirements (such as additional vaccines or lab results), and finally receiving the sealed packet.

How long this takes depends on several factors, including the clinic's current workload, how quickly lab results are returned, and whether any follow-up items are needed. Some clinics can complete the process within a week or two; others may take longer during busy periods.

Because timelines can affect your overall immigration application schedule, it is worth asking the clinic for a realistic estimate of their current turnaround before you book â and confirming whether that estimate assumes everything goes smoothly or accounts for common follow-up scenarios.

Final Thoughts

The USCIS medical exam is a required step that involves more variables than many applicants anticipate. Costs are not standardized, timelines depend on multiple factors, and preparation makes a meaningful difference in how smoothly the process goes.
