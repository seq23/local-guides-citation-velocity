  • Personal injury — what happens in the days after a crash, how a claim moves from report to settlement, the filing deadline in your state, how comparative negligence changes a number, and how a contingency fee is actually calculated. The canonical guide site is theaccidentguides.com.
  • Neuropsychological evaluations — what an ADHD, autism or memory evaluation includes, how it differs from a school IEP evaluation or a plain IQ test, how many sessions to expect, and what the hospital and private routes each cost. The canonical guide site is neuroevalguides.com.
  • Testosterone, hormone and hair-loss care — the symptoms and lab work behind a low-testosterone question, how telehealth and in-person programmes differ in monitoring, how TRT compares with clomiphene and enclomiphene, and the questions to ask before joining any programme. The canonical guide site is hormonesivhair.com.
  • USCIS medical exams — the Form I-693 exam itself, how to find a civil surgeon rather than a panel physician, which vaccination records to bring, and what separates a rejection from a denial or a request for evidence. The canonical guide site is uscisexam.com.

How these guides are written

Each guide states its answer first, then the facts it rests on, with the primary source named and dated next to the claim. Government and regulator pages come before trade bodies, and trade bodies before anything a provider publishes about itself. Nothing here is advice about your own case, no guide ranks or endorses a named provider, and no page quotes a price, an outcome or a timeline unless a cited source states it. Where a source has been updated since a guide was reviewed, the review date on the page is the date to trust.
