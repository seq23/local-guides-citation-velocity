Hospital Discharge to Assisted Living in Memphis: What Discharge Planners Need From Families

Hospital discharge to assisted living in Memphis is the handoff from a hospital discharge planner, who must evaluate needs and send medical information, to a community that must assess and accept your parent. Families supply the choice, payment and signed documents, often within days. See From Hospital to Assisted Living in Memphis.

Direct answer

When a parent leaves Baptist, Methodist, Saint Francis or Regional One for assisted living, the hospital's discharge planner must evaluate post-discharge needs and send necessary medical information to the receiving community. Families supply the decision: which community, its acceptance, payment, and signed documents, often within a day or two.

Decision questions

Discharge planners at Baptist Memorial Hospital-Memphis, Methodist University Hospital, Saint Francis Hospital-Memphis and Regional One Health work to a short clock. They need the family to name the community, confirm it has accepted your parent after its own assessment, confirm how the first month will be paid, and provide contact details for the person with power of attorney or health care authority. Have those four answers ready and the discharge moves faster; From Hospital to Assisted Living in Memphis has the full checklist.

Quick checklist

  • Ask the case manager for the expected discharge date on day one.
  • Name one family contact for the hospital.
  • Have the power of attorney and health care directive ready.
  • Shortlist two communities that can assess quickly.

Red flags

  • No family contact named
  • Waiting for the discharge date before calling communities
  • No copy of the health care directive

Medicare's hospital Conditions of Participation require a discharge planning process that identifies patients likely to need post-discharge care and evaluates their needs. At discharge, the hospital must send necessary medical information, including the course of illness and treatment, post-discharge goals of care and treatment preferences, to the facilities and providers responsible for follow-up care. Ask for your own copy of what is sent, and ask Memphis communities what they need to see.

Quick checklist

  • Ask the hospital what it will send and to whom.
  • Request a copy of the discharge summary.
  • Get the medication list reconciled at discharge.
  • Confirm follow-up appointments.

Red flags

  • No discharge summary given to the family
  • Medication changes not explained
  • No follow-up appointment booked

A community will want to assess your parent, in person or from records, to decide whether it can meet their needs under its license. It usually asks for physician orders and a medication list, recent notes on mobility, cognition and continence, any required tuberculosis screening, and the signed residency agreement and first payment. 40 Questions to Ask on an Assisted Living Tour helps when there is no time for a long tour.

Quick checklist

  • Ask each community for its admission paperwork list.
  • Ask whether it can assess at the hospital.
  • Ask how fast it can accept.
  • Ask what care level it expects to assign.

Red flags

  • Acceptance with no assessment
  • No written price before move-in
  • A care level set without seeing records

Medicare does not pay assisted living rent or care fees, so the first month is usually private pay. TennCare CHOICES can pay for assisted-care services at participating communities once your parent is enrolled, but enrollment takes time. Have the first month's payment and deposit ready, and ask whether the community will accept your parent if they later convert to CHOICES; How Families Pay for Assisted Living covers each route.

Quick checklist

  • Confirm who will pay the first month.
  • Ask about deposits and community fees.
  • Ask whether the community participates in CHOICES.
  • Start any benefit applications now.

Red flags

  • Assuming Medicare covers the move
  • Deposits that are not refundable if acceptance falls through
  • No payment plan in writing
