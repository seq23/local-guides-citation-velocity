West Tennessee vs East Arkansas Senior Living: Licensure and Placement Rules

West Tennessee vs East Arkansas senior living is two licensing systems: Tennessee's Health Facilities Commission licenses assisted-care living facilities and homes for the aged; Arkansas's Office of Long Term Care licenses assisted living at Level I or Level II, and Living Choices pays only in Level II. See Levels of Care Explained: What Tennessee Licenses Each As.

Decision questions

In Shelby County, the Tennessee Health Facilities Commission licenses assisted-care living facilities and residential homes for the aged. Across the river in Crittenden County, which includes West Memphis and Marion, the Arkansas Department of Human Services Office of Long Term Care licenses assisted living facilities. Complaints and inspection records sit with the agency in the community's own state.

Quick checklist

  • Note which state the community is in before comparing.
  • Ask for the license and its level.
  • Find the right state agency for inspection records.
  • Ask the community who to call with a complaint.

Red flags

  • A community that cannot name its licensing agency
  • Comparing license types across states as if they matched
  • No inspection record available

Arkansas licenses assisted living at two levels. Both serve older and disabled adults in a home-like setting; Level II facilities are the ones that may enroll with Arkansas Medicaid as Living Choices Assisted Living providers, and they generally serve residents with higher care needs. Ask any West Memphis community which level it holds before you discuss care or payment.

Quick checklist

  • Ask whether the license is Level I or Level II.
  • Ask what care needs the community can and cannot meet.
  • Ask whether it is a Living Choices provider.
  • Ask what would trigger a move-out.

Red flags

  • A Level I community promising Medicaid payment
  • No written move-out criteria
  • Care promises beyond the license level

Tennessee's assisted-care living facility, or ACLF, accepts primarily aged residents for domiciliary care and may provide a higher level of care, including some medical services. Its residential home for the aged provides room, board and personal services in a smaller setting. ACLF standards are in Tenn. Comp. R. & Regs. chapter 0720-26; Levels of Care Explained: What Tennessee Licenses Each As explains each one in plain language.

Quick checklist

  • Ask a Tennessee community whether it is an ACLF or RHA.
  • Ask what medical services it provides on site.
  • Ask for its latest inspection.
  • Compare with one Arkansas community at the same care level.

Red flags

  • An RHA advertising medical services
  • No inspection record
  • A care plan that is not written down

In Tennessee, TennCare CHOICES pays for assisted-care services at contracted ACLFs. In Arkansas, the Living Choices Assisted Living waiver pays for services in Level II facilities enrolled as providers; it has a limited number of slots and a waiting list forms when they fill. In both states the resident pays room and board, and Medicaid eligibility belongs to the state where your parent lives, whether that is Memphis or Marion.

Quick checklist

  • Decide which state your parent will live in before applying.
  • Ask the Arkansas community about Living Choices slots.
  • Ask the Tennessee community about CHOICES participation.
  • Budget room and board either way.

Red flags

  • Applying for Medicaid in the wrong state
  • Assuming a Level I community can take Living Choices
  • No plan for the waiting period

Use the agency for the community's state. Tennessee communities are inspected by the Health Facilities Commission; Arkansas communities by the DHS Office of Long Term Care. Ask each community for its most recent inspection and plan of correction, and look for repeat findings in the same area. How to Read a Tennessee Assisted Living Inspection and Licensure Record shows what to look for.

Quick checklist

  • Request the latest inspection report.
  • Look for repeat findings.
  • Ask what changed after each finding.
  • Note the inspection date.

Red flags

  • Refusal to share the latest inspection
  • Repeat findings in medication handling
  • An unresolved plan of correction
