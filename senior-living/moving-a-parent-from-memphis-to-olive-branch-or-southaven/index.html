Moving a Parent From Memphis to Olive Branch or Southaven: Cross-State Checklist

Moving a parent from Memphis to Olive Branch or Southaven is a cross-state move: the Mississippi State Department of Health replaces Tennessee's licensing body, Mississippi's Assisted Living Waiver replaces TennCare, and records and benefits must follow. See The Assisted Living Residency Agreement: Fees, Deposits and Discharge Rules.

Direct answer

Moving a parent from Memphis to an assisted living community in Olive Branch or Southaven means changing states: a different licensing body (the Mississippi State Department of Health), a different Medicaid program (Mississippi's Assisted Living Waiver instead of TennCare), and new records, address and benefit changes to file before move-in day.

Decision questions

Three things change at the state line. The licensing body becomes the Mississippi State Department of Health, which licenses personal care homes as either assisted living or residential living. Medicaid help moves from TennCare to the Mississippi Division of Medicaid. And the records, address and benefit notices that pointed to Tennessee all need updating before or right after move-in in Olive Branch or Southaven.

Quick checklist

  • Confirm the community's Mississippi license type.
  • List every benefit and account that uses your parent's Tennessee address.
  • Ask the community for its admission paperwork list a month ahead.
  • Set a move-in date that leaves time for records to arrive.

Red flags

  • A community that cannot name its license type
  • No written admission requirements
  • A move date set before records are requested

No. TennCare is Tennessee's Medicaid program and serves Tennessee residents. After the move, help paying for assisted living services would come through Mississippi's Assisted Living Waiver, which requires the community to be licensed as a Personal Care Home-Assisted Living and approved as a Medicaid provider. The waiver is not an entitlement and can have a waiting list, so ask before you move. How Families Pay for Assisted Living covers private-pay planning in the meantime.

Quick checklist

  • Ask the Mississippi community whether it is an approved Assisted Living Waiver provider.
  • Ask the Division of Medicaid about current waiting times.
  • Plan private pay for the waiting period.
  • Tell TennCare about the change of residence.

Red flags

  • Assuming Medicaid coverage transfers automatically
  • A community that "takes Medicaid" but is not a waiver provider
  • No plan for the months before the waiver starts

Communities generally ask for a current medication list and physician orders, a recent history and physical, any required tuberculosis screening, insurance and Medicare cards, and copies of the power of attorney and health care directive. If your parent is moving straight from a Memphis hospital, the hospital must send necessary medical information to the receiving facility at discharge, but ask for your own copies too; From Hospital to Assisted Living in Memphis lists what discharge planners need from you.

Quick checklist

  • Get the medication list and physician orders signed and dated.
  • Ask the community which screenings it requires and how recent they must be.
  • Copy the power of attorney and health care directive.
  • Ask the community whether it accepts Tennessee-executed documents as they are.

Red flags

  • Outdated medication lists
  • No copy of the power of attorney on file
  • Records sent only by the hospital, with no family copy

Read the agreement for the monthly rate and what it includes, how care levels are set and changed, notice periods for rate increases, what triggers a discharge, and the refund terms for any community fee. Personal care homes in Mississippi follow MSDH minimum standards, but the money terms are in the contract, so get them in writing before you pay a deposit. The Assisted Living Residency Agreement: Fees, Deposits and Discharge Rules walks through each clause.

Quick checklist

  • Find the rate-increase notice period.
  • Find the discharge and move-out clauses.
  • Find the refund terms for deposits and community fees.
  • Ask who to call with a complaint.

Red flags

  • Blank fee lines
  • Discharge allowed "at the community's discretion"
  • A non-refundable fee that is not explained

Olive Branch, Southaven, Horn Lake and Hernando all sit within a short drive of Memphis, so family visits stay practical. Compare communities in one or two of them on license type, care level, price and whether they are approved Assisted Living Waiver providers, then tour your top two.

Quick checklist

  • Pick the DeSoto County city closest to the family member who visits most.
  • Shortlist two communities there.
  • Ask each about waiver participation.
  • Tour both before choosing.

Red flags

  • Choosing on location alone
  • No visit before move-in
  • A community that cannot say whether it is a waiver provider
