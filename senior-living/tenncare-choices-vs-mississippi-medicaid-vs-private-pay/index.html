TennCare CHOICES vs Mississippi Medicaid vs Private Pay for Assisted Living

TennCare CHOICES vs Mississippi Medicaid vs private pay is a question of who pays which part: both Medicaid programs pay care services, not room and board, and only at participating communities; private pay covers everything. TennCare caps 2026 room and board at $2,385.60 a month. See How Families Pay for Assisted Living.

Direct answer

TennCare CHOICES and Mississippi's Assisted Living Waiver both pay for assisted living services, not room and board, and only at communities that contract with them. Private pay covers everything and opens every door. In 2026 TennCare caps the room-and-board charge at $2,385.60 a month for CHOICES members.

Decision questions

CHOICES pays for assisted-care living facility services, the care itself: personal care, homemaker services and medication oversight. It does not pay room and board; federal rules bar Medicaid home and community-based services from covering it. TennCare's payment for those services may not exceed $1,497.77 a month (effective July 1, 2025), and the member pays room and board from their own income or with family help. When the Money Runs Out: Medicaid Spend-Down in Tennessee explains how to apply.

Quick checklist

  • Confirm your parent meets nursing-facility level of care.
  • Ask the community whether it contracts with TennCare for CHOICES.
  • Budget for room and board from your parent's income.
  • Ask how long CHOICES enrollment is taking.

Red flags

  • A community that says TennCare pays "everything"
  • No written room-and-board figure
  • Applying without checking whether the community participates

For 2026, TennCare caps room and board for CHOICES members at the lesser of $2,385.60 a month or what the community charges non-CHOICES residents. That cap is 80% of the $2,982 personal needs allowance. With TennCare's services payment, the most a community can receive is $3,883.37 a month, unless the family chooses to pay extra for a private room, which cannot be a condition of admission. That ceiling is why fewer Memphis communities accept CHOICES.

Quick checklist

  • Ask for the room-and-board charge in writing.
  • Check it does not exceed $2,385.60 a month in 2026.
  • Ask whether a private room is optional.
  • Ask what is included in room and board.

Red flags

  • A private-room upcharge required for admission
  • Room and board above the TennCare cap
  • Extra "care" fees added on top of the CHOICES payment

Mississippi's waiver also pays for services, not room and board. It covers case management, personal care, homemaker services, medication oversight, attendant care and related services in a facility licensed by the Mississippi State Department of Health as a Personal Care Home-Assisted Living and approved as a Medicaid provider, in Southaven or Olive Branch for example. Applicants must be 21 or older and qualify medically and financially, and the program can have a waiting list.

Quick checklist

  • Confirm the community is an approved waiver provider.
  • Ask the Division of Medicaid about the waiting list.
  • Budget room and board from your parent's income.
  • Ask what services the per diem covers at that community.

Red flags

  • Assuming the waiver starts immediately
  • A community that is licensed but not a waiver provider
  • No written room-and-board figure

Private pay works anywhere, starts immediately and lets you choose any community and apartment. It makes sense when your parent has savings, home sale proceeds or long-term care insurance to cover the gap, or when the communities you prefer do not take Medicaid. Plan how long the money lasts at the quoted rate plus expected increases, and pick a community that will accept CHOICES or the waiver later if you may need it. How Families Pay for Assisted Living shows how to build that plan.

Quick checklist

  • Estimate how many months savings will last.
  • Ask whether the community accepts residents who later convert to Medicaid.
  • Check long-term care insurance elimination periods.
  • Check VA pension with Aid and Attendance if your parent served.

Red flags

  • No plan for spend-down
  • A community that will discharge residents who convert to Medicaid
  • Selling the home without checking Medicaid rules first

  • Check whether the plan's network covers doctors near the community.
  • Ask the community which pharmacies it works with.

Red flags

  • Dropping Medicare coverage after the move
