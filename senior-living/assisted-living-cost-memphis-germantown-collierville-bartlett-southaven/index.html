Decision questions

The most recent public benchmark is CareScout's 2025 Cost of Care Survey: a Tennessee median of $67,140 a year, about $5,595 a month, against a national median of $74,400 a year, or $6,200 a month. Rates are collected by metropolitan area, but the published release gives the state figure. Treat it as the middle of the market and expect individual Memphis quotes above and below it.

Quick checklist

  • Use the state median as a benchmark, not a price.
  • Ask each community for its base rate and care fee separately.
  • Ask what the last rate increase was and when.
  • Write down every one-time fee.

Red flags

  • A quote with no care fee shown
  • "All-inclusive" pricing that excludes medication management
  • A verbal quote that will not be put in writing

No public survey publishes separate medians for Germantown, Collierville or Bartlett, so any city ranking you read without a source is a guess. Price follows the community: building age, apartment size, the care level your parent is assessed at and whether memory care is needed. The honest comparison is three written quotes per city, from Germantown, Collierville and Bartlett communities, for the same apartment size and care level.

Quick checklist

  • Request quotes for the same apartment size in each city.
  • Ask each community to quote the same care level.
  • Compare the total monthly price, not the base rate.
  • Ask how often the care level is reassessed.

Red flags

  • City "averages" with no named source
  • A low base rate with care fees disclosed only after assessment
  • Pressure to sign before you have a second quote

CareScout's 2025 survey ranks Mississippi among the lowest-cost states for assisted living, below Tennessee's median. That is a statewide pattern, not a Southaven price. Southaven communities are licensed by the Mississippi State Department of Health as personal care homes, and Medicaid help runs through Mississippi's Assisted Living Waiver rather than TennCare, so compare the whole picture, not only the rate.

Quick checklist

  • Get Southaven quotes for the same care level as your Tennessee quotes.
  • Ask whether the community is licensed as Personal Care Home-Assisted Living.
  • Check how a move across the state line affects your parent's Medicaid plans.
  • Add travel time for family visits into the decision.

Red flags

  • Choosing on a lower rate without checking the license type
  • Assuming TennCare will follow your parent to Mississippi
  • A quote that leaves out the care fee

Most communities price in layers: a base rate for the apartment and meals, a care level or points fee set by an assessment, sometimes medication management, a memory care premium where it applies, and a one-time community fee. Ask for each line in writing and ask how the care level changes when your parent's needs change, because that is where the monthly bill usually grows. The Assisted Living Residency Agreement: Fees, Deposits and Discharge Rules shows where those terms sit in the contract.

Quick checklist

  • Ask for the price sheet with every fee line.
  • Ask how the care level is assessed and by whom.
  • Ask how much notice the community gives before a rate increase.
  • Ask whether the community fee is refundable.

Red flags

  • No written price sheet
  • Care level changes without a new assessment
  • Rate increases with no notice period in the agreement

Medicare does not pay for long-term custodial care in assisted living. In Tennessee, TennCare CHOICES can pay for assisted-care services for eligible members, capped at $1,497.77 a month (effective July 1, 2025), but not for room and board, which the member pays, capped at $2,385.60 a month for 2026. Only communities that contract with TennCare accept that payment; How Families Pay for Assisted Living covers the other routes.

Quick checklist

  • Ask each community whether it accepts TennCare CHOICES.
  • Check whether your parent is a veteran or surviving spouse for VA pension with Aid and Attendance.
  • Ask whether long-term care insurance benefits are accepted and how claims are filed.
  • Plan how many months private pay can last.

Red flags

  • A promise that Medicare will cover assisted living
  • A community that will not say whether it takes TennCare
  • No plan for what happens when savings run low
