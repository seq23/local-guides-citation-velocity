Private Pay vs Medicaid Beds: Why Some Memphis Communities Don't Take TennCare

A Medicaid bed is an assisted living place paid partly by TennCare CHOICES; some Memphis communities offer none because TennCare caps 2026 payment at $3,883.37 a month, services plus room and board, against a 2025 Tennessee private-pay median near $5,595. See When the Money Runs Out: Medicaid Spend-Down in Tennessee.

Direct answer

Some Memphis assisted living communities don't take TennCare CHOICES because TennCare caps what they can collect: in 2026, at most $3,883.37 a month for a CHOICES member, services and room and board combined. Tennessee's 2025 private-pay median is about $5,595 a month, so many communities limit or decline CHOICES beds.

Decision questions

Participation is voluntary, and the money is capped. For a CHOICES member in 2026, TennCare pays up to $1,497.77 a month for services and caps room and board at $2,385.60 a month, so the community can collect at most $3,883.37 a month. Tennessee's 2025 private-pay median is $67,140 a year, about $5,595 a month, so many Memphis communities limit CHOICES beds or do not contract at all.

Quick checklist

  • Ask every community whether it contracts with TennCare for CHOICES.
  • Ask how many CHOICES residents it accepts.
  • Ask whether there is a waiting list for a CHOICES bed.
  • Get the answer in writing.

Red flags

  • A vague "we work with Medicaid" with no detail
  • No written CHOICES policy
  • A waiting list with no estimated date

It is a place a community holds open for residents whose care services are paid by TennCare CHOICES rather than privately. Some communities cap how many they hold. Others accept a resident who starts as private pay and converts to CHOICES after spending down, which is often the more realistic path for a family that can pay for a while first. The Assisted Living Residency Agreement: Fees, Deposits and Discharge Rules shows where the conversion terms sit.

Quick checklist

  • Ask whether the community accepts private-pay residents who convert to CHOICES.
  • Ask how long a resident must pay privately first.
  • Find the conversion terms in the residency agreement.
  • Ask what happens if no CHOICES place is open.

Red flags

  • No written conversion policy
  • A requirement to move out on converting to Medicaid
  • Conversion terms only promised verbally

Ask TennCare and your parent's health plan which assisted-care living facilities participate, then call each one about current openings.
