USCIS Medical Exam Document Checklist: What to Bring

Everything you need to gather before your immigration medical exam so your appointment goes smoothly and your case stays on track.

Be Prepared, Not Stressed

Walking into your USCIS medical exam with the right documents in hand can make the entire experience smoother, faster, and far less stressful. Your civil surgeon, the licensed physician authorized by USCIS to conduct these exams, depends on the documents you bring to confirm your identity and review important health-related information. Coming prepared shows that you take the process seriously and helps avoid delays or the need for a follow-up visit.

Why Your Documents Matter So Much

Civil surgeons are not just performing a routine checkup. They are completing an official government form on your behalf, and accuracy is everything. The documents you bring allow the civil surgeon to verify who you are, understand your vaccination history, and ensure that all required information is captured correctly before submitting paperwork to USCIS. Missing or incomplete documentation at your appointment could mean rescheduling, additional costs, or delays in your overall immigration case. Taking a few minutes to gather your documents ahead of time is one of the simplest ways to protect your application timeline.

The Core Documents You Should Always Bring

While individual clinics may have slightly different requirements, there is a standard set of documents that virtually every civil surgeon's office will expect you to bring. Make sure to have all of the following ready before your appointment:

  • Government-issued photo ID: A passport, state-issued driver's license, or other official identification that includes your photo and full legal name.
  • Appointment confirmation: Any email, letter, or printed confirmation showing your scheduled exam date and time.
  • Vaccination records: Bring any immunization records you have, whether from your home country or the United States. These do not need to be perfect â your civil surgeon will guide you if anything is missing.
  • USCIS notices related to your case: If you have received any official notices from USCIS, such as a receipt notice or an interview appointment letter, bring copies to your exam.

What to Do If You Don't Have Vaccination Records

Many immigrants worry about arriving without a complete vaccination history, and this is completely understandable. Records can be lost, were never issued in a standard format, or may be difficult to obtain from another country. The good news is that you are not expected to have a perfect vaccination file. If your records are incomplete or unavailable, your civil surgeon will explain your options according to current USCIS guidance. This may include receiving certain vaccines during or after your appointment, or documenting your history based on available evidence. Simply be honest about what you have and what you don't â your civil surgeon is there to help you navigate this process, not penalize you for gaps.

Helpful Extras: Medical History and Medications

While not always required, bringing a brief summary of your personal medical history and a list of any current medications can be genuinely useful during your exam. Civil surgeons review your overall health as part of the immigration process, and having this information ready allows the appointment to move more efficiently. You do not need a formal medical record â even a handwritten note listing chronic conditions, past surgeries, or the names of medications you currently take can help your civil surgeon complete the exam accurately and thoroughly.

Payment and Clinic-Specific Instructions

Payment policies vary from clinic to clinic, and it is important to check with your specific civil surgeon's office before your appointment. Some clinics require payment upfront, while others bill after services are completed. Accepted payment methods also differ â some offices accept credit cards and checks, while others may prefer cash. Most clinics will explain their process when you schedule your appointment, but if you are unsure, do not hesitate to call ahead and confirm. Being prepared financially helps you avoid any last-minute surprises on the day of your visit.

What NOT to Bring â Or Touch

One important rule that catches many applicants off guard: if your civil surgeon provides you with a sealed envelope containing your completed medical exam results, do not open it. These sealed documents are meant to be submitted directly to USCIS or brought to your immigration interview exactly as provided. Opening the envelope could invalidate the entire exam and force you to start over. Unless USCIS or your civil surgeon explicitly instructs you to open any document, leave sealed items completely intact.

A Final Word of Guidance

Immigration rules and USCIS requirements can change over time, and what applies to one person's case may differ from another's. This checklist is meant to give you a strong starting point, but always follow the specific instructions provided by your civil surgeon's office. When in doubt, contact your clinic directly or consult a qualified immigration attorney. Only USCIS and your civil surgeon can make official determinations about what is required for your individual case. Being informed, organized, and proactive is the best thing you can do to keep your immigration journey moving forward.
