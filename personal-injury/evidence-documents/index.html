Quick answer

Decision checklist

What evidence and documents matter most after an accident

Use this page as a practical evidence checklist so you know what to save first, what not to send casually, and what you can still rebuild if something is missing.

  1. Preserve scene photos, vehicle/property photos, and contact information early
  2. Keep every medical note, imaging result, discharge paper, and bill
  3. Separate insurer communication from your own record file
  4. Save wage-loss and out-of-pocket proof as it appears
  5. Do not send broad medical-record packages casually without understanding why
Decision framework

What makes evidence useful instead of messy

  • Evidence works best when grouped by type.
  • Medical proof and financial proof usually need separate tracking.
  • Good documentation is about timeline clarity as much as quantity.
  • You do not need every record in hand before talking to counsel.

Scene proof comes first: photos, damage, and basic facts

The first evidence bucket is scene proof: photos of vehicles or conditions, visible injuries, road or property conditions, and any basic identifying information that helps anchor what happened.

Medical proof builds the injury timeline

The next bucket is medical proof: intake notes, discharge papers, imaging, prescriptions, referrals, and bills. These records matter because they show when symptoms were reported and how treatment developed.

You do not need every bill before talking to a lawyer

Quick answers

The first evidence bucket is scene proof: photos of vehicles or conditions, visible injuries, road or property conditions, and any basic identifying information that helps anchor what happened.

Quick checklist

  • Save wide shots and close-ups
  • Keep date-stamped photos if possible
  • Store witness and police or incident details in one place
  • Collect photos of damage, scene conditions, and visible injuries
  • Save names, contact info, and insurance details
  • Get witness names before people disappear
  • Keep medical records and symptom timeline together

Red flags

  • You rely only on memory for the scene
  • Waiting too long to document the scene
  • Sending raw records without understanding what is being requested
  • Assuming the police report alone proves everything
  • Pressure to sign immediately
  • No written fee explanation
