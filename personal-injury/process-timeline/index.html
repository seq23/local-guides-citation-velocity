How Long Does a Personal Injury Lawsuit Take?

Phase-by-phase personal injury timing, settlement stages, and what creates delay.

Quick answer

Quick answer

A personal injury case is easier to understand as phases instead of one single number. Intake and investigation may take days to a few weeks, treatment and case development can take weeks or months, pre-suit negotiation can take weeks to months, and litigation can add many more months if the case does not settle early.

Direct answers

How Long Does a Personal Injury Lawsuit Take From Start to Finish?

The easiest way to understand timing is to break the case into phases instead of asking for one single number. Intake and investigation may take days to a few weeks, treatment and case development can take weeks or months, pre-suit negotiation can take weeks to months, and litigation can add many more months if the case does not settle early.

What Happens During Each Phase of a Personal Injury Case

After you sign, the usual next steps are investigation, records collection, insurance notice, treatment tracking, and claim-building. If the case does not settle pre-suit, the timeline shifts into filing, discovery, motions, negotiation, and possibly trial preparation.

When Should You Talk to a Lawyer After an Accident?

It usually helps to talk to a lawyer before recorded statements, early settlement pressure, or deadline confusion turn a simple claim into a mess. Early contact matters most when fault is disputed, injuries are getting clearer over time, or the insurer is already pushing for quick statements or releases.
