Decision checklist

How to choose a personal injury lawyer

Most people make a better choice when they compare a few non-negotiables before talking about “best” or “top rated.”

  1. Confirm the lawyer handles your accident type
  2. Get the contingency fee and case costs in writing
  3. Ask who will actually run the file day to day
  4. Compare trial readiness, not just ad visibility
  5. Talk to 2–3 firms before signing
Decision framework

What usually drives the decision

  • Case-type fit matters more than slogans.
  • Fee structure and costs can change your net payout.
  • Trial leverage and communication quality usually matter more than review volume.

Direct answers

Start with case-type fit and plaintiff-side focus

Start by confirming the lawyer is plaintiff-side and regularly handles your accident type. A general litigator or intake-heavy ad firm is not the same as someone who routinely handles the exact claim pattern you have.

Compare firms using a real shortlist, not “best” claims

Treat “best” as a filtering question, not a verdict. The useful comparison is usually lawyer type, communication style, fee clarity, and whether the firm can explain your next steps without pressuring you to sign immediately.

Use the consultation to see who will actually handle the case

A free consultation is useful when it tells you who will manage the file, how updates work, and whether the firm can explain your likely process without overpromising outcomes.

Provider call script (simple)

Use this short script when you call a clinic or office. Keep notes.

  • Ask cost range
  • Ask what’s included
  • Ask earliest appointment
  • Ask cancellation policy
  • Ask who you’ll see
Script

Questions to ask any provider before booking

These questions help you compare options fast without getting sold to.

  • What is the total cost?
  • What’s included?
  • What are the next steps?
  • What happens if I need follow-up?
  • How do you handle refunds/cancellations?

How to read online reviews (quick rules)

One bad review is normal. Patterns matter. Look for repeated complaints about billing, follow-up, or safety.

  • Look for patterns
  • Watch for billing issues
  • Check recent reviews
  • Confirm licensing
