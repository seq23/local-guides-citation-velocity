Personal Injury: Cost & Fees

Costs, contingency fees, and what to ask before you sign.

Quick answer

Personal injury fees are usually contingency-based, but the real comparison is the percentage, case costs, medical liens, and how the final payout is calculated. The contract details that look small on day one can materially change what you keep at the end.

Decision checklist

How to compare injury-lawyer costs without getting surprised later

Use this page to understand what you may owe, what comes out of the settlement, and how to sanity-check fee math before you sign.

  1. Get the contingency percentage and escalation triggers in writing
  2. Ask how case costs are advanced and when they are repaid
  3. Ask how liens, bills, and reimbursements affect the payout
  4. Ask what happens if the case is lost, dismissed, or transferred
  5. Run one sample payout scenario before signing
Decision framework

What usually changes your net recovery

  • The fee percentage is only one line item.
  • Case costs, liens, and timing affect what you actually keep.
  • A lower headline fee can still produce a worse net result if the contract is vague.
  • You should be able to explain the payout waterfall in plain English before you sign.

Direct answers

Understand the contingency fee before you compare percentages

Most firms work on contingency, but the useful comparison is the written percentage, whether it changes after filing, and whether any situations trigger a higher fee later in the case.

Case costs and records can change the real total

Case costs are separate from the attorney fee. Ask what the firm advances, what you repay, and whether costs come out before or after the attorney fee is calculated.

Run the payout math with a simple worked example

Do one sample payout check before signing. Example: if a settlement is $100,000, the attorney fee is 33%, case costs are $6,000, and liens are $12,000, your real take-home is very different from the headline number. A good firm can walk you through that logic without getting defensive.
