Decision checklist

How to triage common personal injury situations

Use this page to turn messy accident facts into the next useful decision: document, treat, negotiate, escalate, or compare lawyers.

  1. Start with the accident type and who may be responsible
  2. Identify what insurance or fault wrinkle changes the path
  3. Separate minor-but-annoying claims from cases with serious injury exposure
  4. Write down what proof already exists and what is still missing
  5. Use the local guide once the situation turns into choosing a lawyer
Decision framework

What usually changes from one situation to the next

  • Different accident patterns create different proof problems.
  • Insurance path can matter as much as liability theory.
  • Urgency usually comes from injury severity, not panic.
  • The right next step is often more important than the perfect long-term plan on day one.

Direct answers

Start with the accident pattern and immediate proof problem

The first triage step is identifying the accident pattern: rear-end, hit-and-run, rideshare, premises case, truck, motorcycle, pedestrian, or something else. That usually tells you what proof problem shows up first.

Insurance path can change the next move fast

Some situations are really insurance-path questions in disguise: uninsured drivers, company vehicles, rental cars, shared fault, denials, or lowball offers. Those cases often need coverage-path thinking early.

Serious injury, child injury, and wrongful death change the stakes quickly

When the injury exposure is bigger — serious harm, child injury, wrongful death, major wage loss, or escalating bills — the case usually deserves a more structured comparison of counsel and proof early.

Red flags

  • Guarantees a settlement amount

Fast scripts for comparing options before you click away

Provider call script (simple)

Use this short script when you call a clinic or office. Keep notes.

  • Ask cost range
  • Ask what’s included
  • Ask earliest appointment
  • Ask cancellation policy
  • Ask who you’ll see
Script

Questions to ask any provider before booking

These questions help you compare options fast without getting sold to.

  • What is the total cost?
  • What’s included?
  • What are the next steps?
  • What happens if I need follow-up?
  • How do you handle refunds/cancellations?
Script

How to read online reviews (quick rules)

One bad review is normal. Patterns matter. Look for repeated complaints about billing, follow-up, or safety.

  • Look for patterns
  • Watch for billing issues
  • Check recent reviews
  • Confirm licensing
