Quick answer

Decision checklist

How to handle insurance and fault questions after an accident

Use this page to slow down insurer communication, understand shared-fault rules, and identify which coverage path actually matters before you say too much.

  1. Do not give a recorded statement casually
  2. Ask what your state does with shared fault before assuming the claim is dead
  3. Separate your insurer from the other driver’s insurer when you think through strategy
  4. Ask what uninsured or underinsured coverage may apply
  5. Write down every insurer contact and every deadline
Decision framework

What usually drives insurance and fault outcomes early

  • Early statements can lock in bad framing.
  • State fault rules change whether partial blame destroys or only reduces recovery.
  • Coverage path matters as much as fault theory when the other driver lacks insurance.

Direct answers

Recorded statements are not routine small talk

A recorded statement can become evidence. Before agreeing, know which insurer is asking, what the purpose is, and whether you understand the facts well enough to answer without guessing.

Shared-fault rules are not the same in every state

Partial fault does not mean the claim automatically disappears. Some states reduce recovery, some block it at a threshold, and a few are much harsher. This is where state-specific rules actually matter.

Your insurer and the other driver’s insurer do different jobs

Do not collapse all insurance calls into one category. Your insurer, the other driver’s insurer, and any UM/UIM coverage can create different strategy choices and different risks.
