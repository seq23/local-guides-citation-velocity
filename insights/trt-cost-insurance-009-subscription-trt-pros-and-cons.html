What this answer is based on

TRT cost is easier to compare when you break it into a cost-by-method table instead of chasing one headline number. Injections are often the lowest monthly medication cost but still add labs and follow-up visits; gels can cost more monthly but may feel simpler; pellets may front-load cost and procedure fees. Always compare insurance coverage, out-of-pocket cost, startup labs, repeat labs, follow-ups, and realistic annual cost before you call one option cheaper. Quick checklist

  • Injections: compare monthly medication cost, supplies, repeat labs, and follow-up visit fees
  • Gels: compare monthly medication price, insurance coverage, refill friction, and follow-up costs
  • Pellets: compare insertion fee, repeat procedure timing, monitoring cost, and total annual cost
  • Insurance scenario: ask what is covered, what needs prior authorization, and what still lands on you
  • Annual cost: add startup workup, repeat labs, medication, visits, and any add-on fees

Red flags to watch

  • One-number pricing with no method breakdown
  • “Insurance may cover it” without specifics
  • No annual cost estimate
  • No explanation of extra lab or follow-up fees
  • Price pitch before treatment method is explained
