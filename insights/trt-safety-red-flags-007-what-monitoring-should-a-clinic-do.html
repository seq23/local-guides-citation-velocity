What monitoring should a clinic do?

Direct answer

For “What monitoring should a clinic do”: structure this as risk stratification instead of one long fear list. Low-risk profiles still need real monitoring, medium-risk profiles need closer follow-up, and higher-risk profiles need more caution around blood pressure, sleep apnea, fertility, blood counts, or prior medical history. The most useful answer is not ‘TRT is safe’ or ‘TRT is risky’—it is ‘what raises or lowers risk in this patient profile?’.

Quick checklist

  • Low-risk profile: stable baseline labs, good follow-up, and no obvious pressure points ignored
  • Medium-risk profile: symptoms or history that need tighter monitoring and clearer dose-change rules
  • Higher-risk profile: blood pressure, sleep, fertility, hematocrit, or other history that changes the plan
  • Monitoring rule: know what will be checked, when, and what triggers action
  • Provider rule: know how the clinic handles side effects rather than hoping nothing changes

Red flags to watch

  • Treats every patient profile the same
  • No monitoring schedule
  • Minimizes blood pressure, sleep, or fertility issues
  • No action thresholds for abnormal labs
  • Safety talk only appears after payment
