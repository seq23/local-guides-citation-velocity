Important context

TRT and fertility what to ask

Raise fertility before you start, because testosterone therapy suppresses the body's own testosterone production, and with it sperm production — this is an expected effect of the treatment rather than an unusual side effect. If you may want children, that conversation changes what you are offered: there are alternative approaches intended to raise testosterone without the same suppression, and there is the option of banking sperm beforehand. Recovery after stopping varies between individuals and is not guaranteed, which is precisely why the discussion belongs before the first prescription.

Copy-ready script

What to ask before starting, if you may want children

Ask these before the first prescription. After starting, the options narrow.

  1. I may want children — how does this treatment affect that?
  2. Should I have a semen analysis before starting?
  3. What alternatives are there that are intended to raise testosterone without the same suppression of sperm production?
  4. If I start this treatment, what is the plan if I decide to try for children later?
  5. What is known about recovery after stopping, for someone with my history and age?
  6. Should I bank sperm first, and what does that involve and cost?
  7. Can you refer me to a urologist or fertility specialist before I decide?
  8. How would we monitor this if I go ahead?

Checklist

Why this conversation has to come first

The sequence matters more here than in most treatment decisions.

  • Suppression of sperm production is an expected consequence of testosterone therapy, not a rare complication.
  • A baseline semen analysis is only a baseline if it is taken before treatment starts.
  • Alternatives intended to preserve fertility are easiest to consider before a prescription is written.
  • Sperm banking is straightforward beforehand and more complicated afterwards.
  • Recovery after stopping varies between individuals, so it cannot be relied on as the plan.
  • If a clinic does not raise fertility during your evaluation, raise it yourself — and treat the omission as information about the clinic.

Quick checklist

  • Tell the clinician before starting if you may want children, even if not soon
  • Ask whether a baseline semen analysis is appropriate for you
  • Ask about alternatives intended to preserve fertility
  • Ask about sperm banking and what it costs
  • Ask what monitoring of fertility looks like while on treatment
  • Ask what is known about recovery after stopping, for someone with your history
  • Involve your partner in the conversation if family planning is shared
  • Ask for a referral to a fertility specialist or urologist if the answers stay general
  • Monitoring: what labs are checked, when, and what changes the plan
  • Pricing: startup cost, monthly cost, refill policy, and extra lab fees
  • Lab frequency: baseline, early follow-up, and steady-state monitoring schedule
  • Fertility guidance: whether sperm banking, HCG, or family-planning goals are discussed before treatment
  • Follow-up policy: how side effects, dose changes, and missed labs are handled

Red flags to watch

  • Fertility is not raised at all during your evaluation
  • You are told the effect is fully reversible without qualification
  • No alternative is mentioned when you say you want children
  • No baseline testing is offered before starting
  • The clinic will not refer you to a specialist
  • Starts treatment before reviewing baseline labs
  • Quotes one number but cannot explain total annual cost
  • No fertility discussion when family-planning still matters
  • No clear follow-up policy for side effects or dose changes
  • Promises fast results without monitoring
