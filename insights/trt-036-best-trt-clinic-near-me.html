Copy-ready script

The identical question set to send every candidate

Same questions, in writing, to all of them. The comparison only works if the inputs match.

  1. What blood testing is required before you will issue a prescription?
  2. If a result is out of range, do you repeat it before starting treatment?
  3. Who prescribes, what is their licence type, and in which state are they licensed?
  4. What is the follow-up schedule, and what is measured at each point?
  5. What happens if my results move out of range?
  6. What is the total cost for twelve months including labs?
  7. Will you share results with my own physician?
  8. How do I cancel, and what is the discontinuation plan?

Why proximity comes last

This is decision-support for talking to a clinician. It is not medical advice, and it does not replace evaluation by a licensed physician who knows your history.

  • The number of in-person visits actually required is usually small — establish that number before weighting distance.
  • Search ranking and 'top rated' badges reflect marketing spend and review volume, not clinical safeguards.
  • A clinic that skips baseline testing is not made safer by being nearby.
  • Lab draws can often be done locally even when the clinic is not.
  • Ask your own physician whether they will coordinate — that can matter more than the clinic's address.

Quick checklist

  • Decide the distance you would genuinely travel before you start searching
  • Shortlist at least three rather than comparing one against nothing
  • Send every candidate the identical question set in writing
  • Verify each prescriber's licence with the state medical board
  • Confirm baseline testing is required before prescribing
  • Confirm follow-up lab intervals are fixed
  • Compare twelve-month totals including labs
  • Use proximity to break a tie, not to make the choice
  • Monitoring: what labs are checked, when, and what changes the plan
  • Pricing: startup cost, monthly cost, refill policy, and extra lab fees
  • Lab frequency: baseline, early follow-up, and steady-state monitoring schedule
  • Fertility guidance: whether sperm banking, HCG, or family-planning goals are discussed before treatment
  • Follow-up policy: how side effects, dose changes, and missed labs are handled

Red flags to watch

  • Choosing on proximity or ranking position alone
  • 'Top rated' claims with no stated basis
  • A prescription offered before any blood test
  • No named prescribing clinician
  • Follow-up labs described as optional
  • Pricing quoted monthly with labs unmentioned
  • Starts treatment before reviewing baseline labs
  • Quotes one number but cannot explain total annual cost
  • No fertility discussion when family-planning still matters
  • No clear follow-up policy for side effects or dose changes
  • Promises fast results without monitoring
