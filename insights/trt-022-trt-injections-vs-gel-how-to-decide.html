Important context

TRT injections vs gel how to decide

Decide on three practical things rather than on which is better: whether you can live with the routine, who else in your household could be exposed, and what your insurance and clinic actually support. Gels are applied daily and carry a documented risk of transferring testosterone to another person through skin contact — which matters most in a household with children or a pregnant partner. Injections are less frequent but require you to be comfortable with the process. Bring your household situation and your tolerance for routine to the conversation; the clinical suitability is your prescriber's call.

Protocol

If a gel is prescribed, the precautions that protect other people

These come from the product labelling and exist because transfer to another person through skin contact is a documented risk.

  1. Apply to the site your prescriber specifies, and only that site.
  2. Wash your hands thoroughly straight after applying.
  3. Let the site dry fully before dressing.
  4. Keep the site covered with clothing.
  5. Avoid skin-to-skin contact at the application site with others, particularly children and a pregnant partner.
  6. Wash the site before any contact where transfer is possible.
  7. Tell the prescriber immediately if a child or partner develops unexpected symptoms.
Copy-ready script

What to ask when choosing between them

These get you a recommendation matched to your circumstances rather than to the clinic's default.

  1. Given my situation, which option do you recommend and why that one?
  2. I live with [children / a pregnant partner] — how does that change the recommendation?
  3. How often would I administer this, and what does that involve day to day?
  4. What precautions would I need to keep up?
  5. Does monitoring differ between the options?
  6. What does each cost, and which does my insurance cover?
  7. If this one does not suit me, what does switching involve?
